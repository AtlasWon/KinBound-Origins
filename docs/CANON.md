# KinBound — Canon

The authority on names, people, places and story beats. Anything in the game
that contradicts this file is a bug. Derived from the player's design guide;
where the guide and the shipped game disagreed, the guide wins.

Written for whoever is implementing. Nothing here is dialogue — dialogue is
written at implementation time from the personalities and beats below, in short
1–3 sentence boxes. The story is told through exploration, ruins, short scenes
and the world changing, not through speeches.

## People

**[PLAYER]** — 16. Only child. Lives with Mira in Hearthmere. Father died when
they were 5. Has wanted to be a Kin Trainer their whole childhood. Sixteen is
the age Caelora lets you travel alone, so this birthday is the whole point.

**MIRA** — the player's mother. Mixed feelings: exploring was Elias' great
passion and she is afraid of the same road, but will not let her fear run her
child's life. Gives the player the Tideheart before they leave.

**ELIAS** — the father. Archaeologist and Kin historian; studied how ancient
people lived with Kin. Went on an overseas expedition when the player was 5,
discovered something, secretly posted an artifact home, and died when his
vessel was lost in a storm. No body. **He was murdered** — a Meridian commander
sabotaged the vessel — but nobody knows that yet.

**TARIN** — 16, from Hearthmere, best friend since forever. Competitive,
confident, funny, loyal, a bit reckless. Jokes constantly; goes quiet when it
is serious. **Never a cruel rival and never betrays the player.** His arc runs
parallel: starts wanting to be strong, learns that strong means protecting
people. Ends as one of the best Trainers in Caelora.

**PROFESSOR SORRELL** — ~60. Studies the relationship between Kin and humans.
Knew Elias. Gives out the starters. Later researches Elias' notes and finds the
three Aurelian sites.

**DR. CASSIAN VEYL** — 45–50. Leads the Meridian Order. Intelligent, calm,
respected, wealthy, charismatic, patient. **Must not read as a villain when
first met.** Lost his little sister to a hurricane as a child and has been
trying to stop disasters ever since. His argument is genuinely reasonable —
people only accept disasters because they never had the power to stop them —
and his failure is that wanting to protect everyone became wanting to control
everything. Worked with Elias; they found the Tideheart together. Did not order
Elias' death, but covered it up afterwards, which is what makes him guilty.

**LYRA VEYL** — 17. Cassian's daughter, though the player does not learn that
at once. Works with Meridian, appears helpful and a little mysterious. Her arc
is doubt: she believes in her father, sees Meridian attack a teenager, keeps
finding worse, and finally chooses against him. Rebuilds Meridian afterwards.

**THE MERIDIAN COMMANDER** — the one who sabotaged Elias' vessel. Refuses to
stop at the climax and overrides Cassian. Hardest villain battle in the game.
Arrested afterwards.

## The Meridian Order

Publicly **THE MERIDIAN FOUNDATION** — labs, archaeology, weather stations, Kin
conservation, museums, ocean research, disaster relief. Most employees are
genuinely doing good work and have no idea. The **Order** is hidden inside it.

The Order wants to wake Neravoss and control it with Aurelian technology, and
so control the weather: rain in droughts, hurricanes turned aside, currents
steered, enormous power generated. Whoever holds Neravoss effectively holds the
region, and the leadership grows to want that.

**PROJECT ABYSS CROWN** — the plan. Find Neravoss, wake it with the Tideheart,
ring it with modified Aurelian control devices, force it to move weather.

## The Tideheart

Palm-sized. Deep blue-turquoise core in an ancient metal frame, circular and
spiral markings, something moving inside like liquid or light. Half machine,
half something else.

It is **not a key**. The Aurelians built it to *talk* to Neravoss. It can open
their ruins, wake their mechanisms, play back what they recorded, point toward
their structures, reach Neravoss, and eventually calm it. Meridian believe it
can also be used to control it. They are wrong, and that is the story.

The player does not learn its name at once. It starts as "the thing Dad sent
home".

---

## Neravoss

Ancient Tide legendary, asleep under the sea off Caelora for centuries.
Enormous. Deep-sea serpent crossed with a whale, a manta and a leviathan: huge
fins, long tail, dark ocean colours, bright glowing markings, crown-like growths
around the head.

It is naturally tied to currents, pressure, storms and rain. Calm, it nudges the
sea gently. Frightened or enraged, it makes hurricanes, tsunamis, waterspouts,
floods — without meaning to.

**Neravoss is not evil.** This is the centre of the whole game. It is dangerous
only because people try to hold it down.

## The Aurelians and the Great Deluge

Thousands of years ago an advanced civilisation, the **AURELIANS**, lived across
Caelora and built the ruins. They understood Kin deeply and built technology
that worked through energy and resonance. They found Neravoss and lived
alongside it peacefully — until their leaders tried to make it move the weather
for them. The machines hurt and terrified it. It reacted.

**THE GREAT DELUGE** — the storms and tsunamis that followed destroyed them.
The survivors worked out their mistake, and rather than trying to kill Neravoss
they calmed it. It went back under the sea to sleep. They made the Tideheart so
that whoever came next could talk to it instead of chaining it.

The player should assemble this slowly, out of ruins and recordings.

## Themes

**BOND VS CONTROL** — the title's meaning. Meridian think power is control. The
player proves it is trust. Every major beat should touch this.

**LEGACY** — the player follows Elias' footsteps to learn who he was, and has
to end up somewhere he never went. His journey started theirs; the achievement
is theirs.

## The region

you go. **Not open world** — connected routes and settlements, but routes are
big and believable, never corridors.

### The three Aurelian sites

Found by Sorrell in Elias' notes, alongside the Crest hunt.

1. **EMBERCOIL TEMPLE** (volcanic) — Neravoss existed; ancient people tried to
   control it; first fragment of Elias' recording: he knew, Cassian wanted to
   wake it, he thought it was a mistake.
2. **MIREHAVEN SANCTUM** (wetlands) — the Great Deluge was *caused* by the
   attempt to control, not by Neravoss. Meridian are repeating it.
3. **FROSTMERE OBSERVATORY** (snow) — Elias' final message. Never control it;
   the Tideheart is for talking; force terrifies it; the only safe answer is
   trust. Emotional peak. Let music and picture carry it, not dialogue.

## Act structure

**ACT 1 — leaving.** Wake, Mira gives the Tideheart (it reacts once, nobody
knows why), Sorrell's lab, starter, Tarin's first battle. Route 1. Briarbell and
the first Crest. Route 2's forest ruin: the Tideheart opens it, carvings show
people, Kin, sea and something enormous — and **a Meridian scout sees it**. That
is how they learn Elias' child has it.

**ACT 2 — watched.** Stonewake: museum, Lyra, Terra Hall. Eastwind Ridge, with
Meridian equipment moving toward the coast. Tideglass: Cassian in public, says
he and Elias found the Tideheart together. Tide Hall. Then agents try to take
it, at night; Lyra intervenes, appalled, and admits Meridian want it.

**ACT 3 — the past.** Sorrell finds the three sites. Emberfall, Flame Hall,
Embercoil Temple and the first recording. The wetlands, Mirehaven, Venom Hall,
and the Sanctum — Tarin walks it with the player, they meet the commander, and
learn Elias "stole" the Tideheart from Meridian. Second truth: the Deluge was
human-made.

**ACT 4 — the truth.** Aureline. Lyra gets them into headquarters. Project Abyss
Crown. The records on Elias: sabotage, and Cassian's cover-up. Cassian arrives
and argues his case honestly. Meridian take the Tideheart.

**ACT 5 — the storm.** Frostmere, Frost Crest, the Observatory and Elias' last
message. Skyreach, Gale Crest. The weather turns across the whole region and
**every settlement's dialogue changes**. Crownspire, eighth Crest. The Temple of
the Deep: a real operation with Tarin, Lyra, Sorrell, Hallkeepers. Cassian wakes
Neravoss, the restraints go on, the storm starts, the commander overrides him,
the player beats her, Cassian gives the Tideheart back.

**CLIMAX.** Neravoss free, terrified, furious. Four phases: survive; break the
restraints still on it; use the Tideheart; reach it. The starter appears at the
end — the proof that a bond is not a chain. It calms. It leaves. **It cannot be
caught here.**

**AFTER.** Meridian exposed and investigated. The commander arrested. Cassian
takes responsibility. Lyra rebuilds Meridian as conservation, ethical
archaeology and disaster research. Then the personal ending: the Ascent, Tarin's
last battle, the four Masters, the Champion. **Champion of Caelora.**

**ENDING.** Home to Hearthmere, deliberately mirroring the opening. Mira.
Tarin. The Tideheart goes among Elias' things. The player looks at a map of
Averra and sees how small Caelora is.

**POST-CREDITS.** Underwater. Neravoss passes a carving of several enormous Kin
— itself among them. One of the others starts to glow.

**POSTGAME.** Return to the Temple. Neravoss comes back on its own, knowing the
player. No storm, no machines, no Meridian. Battle it properly, and it chooses
to come with you. The text must say it *chose*.

## Rules for implementers

- **Short dialogue.** 1–3 sentence boxes. No five-minute speeches. Lore lives in
  ruins, documents, notes, NPCs and pictures.
- **Freedom between beats.** The story gives purpose; it must not interrupt the
  adventure. Let people wander, catch, level, detour and come back.
- **Tarin every 1–2 settlements**, never absent for half the game. He has his
  own journey off-screen: NPCs mention he beat a Hallkeeper this morning, or
  lost badly, or caught something rare, or caused trouble. Sometimes he has the
  Crest before you; sometimes he complains you got there first.
- **Settlements need structure** — population, economy, architecture, terrain,
  history, local Kin, a landmark, roads, homes, shops, a clinic. A village must
  feel small; a city must *look* enormous, using decorative buildings that never
  open.
- **Routes are never corridors.** Every major route: a main path, an optional
  branch, a rare-encounter spot, a hidden item, several Trainers, a landmark,
  environmental storytelling, and one reason to come back later with a
  traversal ability.
- **The world responds.** Bright and calm at first; ruins and Meridian in the
  middle; unstable weather later; the whole region reacting near the climax;
  afterwards, damage and rebuilding.

## Build order

The region is roughly five times the size of what is built. This is staged so
each stage is a complete, playable thing rather than a sketch of everything.

- **Stage 1 — the spine.** The rename, the Tideheart, Hearthmere rebuilt to
  spec, Route 1, Briarbell and Hall 1, Route 2's ruin and the Meridian scout,
  Stonewake and Hall 2 with Lyra, and Tarin's rival system. **Act 1 end to end.**
- **Stage 2 — the coast.** Eastwind Ridge, Tideglass, Hall 3, Cassian's public
  appearance, the night theft, Lyra's first doubt. **Act 2.**
- **Stage 3 — the past.** Embercoil, Emberfall, Hall 4, the wetlands, Mirehaven,
  Hall 5, the Sanctum with Tarin. **Act 3.**
- **Stage 4 — the capital.** The central road, Aureline, Meridian HQ, the truth
  about Elias, the theft of the Tideheart. **Act 4.**
- **Stage 5 — the storm.** Frostmere, Hall 6, the Observatory, Skyreach, Hall 7,
  the changing weather, Crownspire, Hall 8. **Act 5.**
- **Stage 6 — the climax.** Temple of the Deep, Neravoss, the aftermath.
- **Stage 7 — the dream.** The Ascent, Tarin's last battle, the Masters, the
  Champion, the ending, the post-credits.
- **Stage 8 — the rest.** Optional locations, the postgame Neravoss quest, side
  quests, the long tail.
